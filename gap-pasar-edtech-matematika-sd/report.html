Ringkasan Eksekutif

Gap-nya nyata dan belum tertutup. Sembilan produk kompetitor (Ruangguru, Zenius, Quipper, CoLearn, QANDA, Kumon, Khan Academy Kids, IXL, Photomath) tidak mendiagnosis jenis kesalahan — semua berhenti di level skor atau topic-mastery. Eedi paling dekat tapi berbasis MCQ-distractor, bukan proses kerja anak.

Sparks Math — kompetitor offline Indonesia — sudah memakai bahasa B/K/H di materi marketing mereka tapi tidak punya instrumen untuk mengukurnya: sinyal permintaan pasar yang bersih, konsep tidak butuh edukasi pasar, hanya butuh instrumen.

Riset stroke/handwriting analytics menunjukkan pendekatan input tulisan tangan valid secara akademik (AIED 2026, Kyoto LEAF/BookRoll) tapi belum pernah diterapkan untuk klasifikasi jenis-kesalahan pada anak SD — persis di titik kosong itulah OSN berada.

Ruangguru

Produk Kompetitor Sebagian
Wilayah Asal: Indonesia (Jakarta-based; also operates in Vietnam and Thailand)Usia Target: Kindergarten through Grade 12 (roughly ages 5-18), plus alumni/adults. ElementarModa Layanan: hybrid - primarily an online app/web platform (ruangbelajar video + quizzes, AiR

Ruangguru is the maximal incumbent - largest scale in Indonesia (15M+ registered users reported as of June 2025), complete Bahasa Indonesia localization, SD Grade 4 math coverage, a direct-to-child AI tutor (AiRIS), a free AI answer engine (Roboguru), adaptive video (Adapto), and a dedicated parent app - and yet it has NO error-type diagnosis. Its entire diagnostic ceiling is the topic-mastery map: 'which chapter is weak'. Three concrete gaps follow. (1) DIAGNOSIS TYPE: nothing in the stack distinguishes a reading error from a concept error from a calculation error; a child who consistently computes correctly but misreads word problems and a child with a genuine place-value misconception receive the identical output ('weak in Topic X') and the identical remediation (rewatch the topic video). (2) INPUT MODALITY: the platform never sees the child's own working. Adapto reads a multiple-choice tap and a self-reported 'Bingung' button; Roboguru/AiRIS read the QUESTION photo, not the child's handwritten attempt. Self-reported confusion is the weakest possible diagnostic signal for a 9-10 year old, who frequently cannot tell that they misunderstood. (3) PARENT ROLE: the parent is a payer and a passive dashboard viewer receiving grades and topic lists over WhatsApp; they are never handed a diagnosis they can act on. The strategic implication for a Grade 4 math error-type product: do not compete on content library, video quality, brand, or the existence of an AI chat - Ruangguru wins all four decisively. Compete on the diagnostic layer (capturing and classifying the child's actual working into a Reading/Concept/Calculation taxonomy) and on converting the parent from dashboard-observer to informed coach - both of which the incumbent has left structurally empty despite a decade of adaptive-learning investment.

Granularitas Diagnosis

topic-mastery-map. Ruanguji/tryout returns per-topic strength/weakness analysis and reinforcement recommendations; ruangbelajar returns a per-subject report card (rapor) with overall and per-subject grades plus 'which missions/topics to complete next'. This is one step above score-only and one step above trouble-spot-quantity, but well below mcq-distractor-mapped-misconception and far below a full error-type taxonomy.

Modalitas Input

mcq-distractor (in-video Adapto checkpoint questions, latihan soal, and tryout multiple-choice items drive the adaptive branching and the topic analysis) + self-report (the explicit 'Paham'/'Bingung' button inside Adapto videos is a self-declared comprehension signal, not an inferred one) + free-response-ocr in a limited sense (Roboguru and AiRIS accept a photographed/typed problem, but they read the QUESTION, not the child's handwritten working, so the child's actual error steps are never captured). Handwriting-stroke capture and analysis of the child's own written solution: absent.

Peran Orang Tua

gatekeeper + dashboard-only. Gatekeeper: the parent is the paying buyer of the ruangbelajar/Privat subscription and the one who installs the app on the (often shared) phone. Dashboard-only thereafter: Ruangguru ships a dedicated parent application, 'Ruangguru - Parent Center' (Google Play package com.ruangguru.parentapp), letting parents monitor what was covered in each session, read tutor notes, and follow progress for both online and offline branch programs; ruangbelajar additionally produces a monthly Laporan Belajar / 'rapor' with overall and per-subject grades and improvement recommendations, retrievable by texting 'Rapor Anak' to Ruangguru's WhatsApp number. Critically, the parent receives NUMBERS AND TOPIC NAMES, not an actionable explanation of the child's error pattern, and the parent is given no role in the remediation loop - they observe, they do not coach.

AI Bicara Langsung ke Anak

yes - unambiguously. AiRIS (personas 'Aurora' and 'Orion') is marketed as Indonesia's first smart interactive AI tutor and is positioned as a 24/7 peer/friend the child talks to directly: learning concepts, doing homework, practice questions, and even off-topic chat about hobbies and future majors. It is available to SD, SMP, and SMA students via the Ruangguru app and ai.ruangguru.com. Roboguru similarly answers the child's photographed/typed questions directly via the app, roboguru.ruangguru.com, and a WhatsApp number, free of charge and across all levels including SD. The AI-to-child channel is therefore fully occupied by the incumbent; it is not a market gap.

Lokalisasi Bahasa

Full native Bahasa Indonesia across the entire stack: UI, teacher-presented video lessons, question banks, AI tutor conversation (AiRIS/Roboguru answer in Indonesian), and parent-facing reports delivered in Indonesian via the Parent Center app and WhatsApp. Localization is not a differentiator gap here - it is complete.

Model Harga

freemium + subscription. Free tier: Roboguru question-answering (app, web, WhatsApp) and AiRIS 'Basic Mode'; paid tier: AiRIS 'Stellar Mode' metered by 'Stellar Energy' credits, and ruangbelajar sold as duration-based subscription packages (3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 1.5 years, 2 years, 2.5 years). ruangbelajar SD is advertised at roughly IDR 300,000-plus per package after a heavy promotional discount; longer durations lower the per-day cost. Private tutoring (Ruangguru Privat) is priced per session/package separately.

Kekuatan Bukti

moderate - the claim that Ruangguru does NOT do error-type diagnosis rests on convergent negative evidence across the official product pages (ruangbelajar SD, ruanguji, airis, llm-info), the vendor's own LLM-facing information page, and multiple independent Indonesian press reports on Adapto and Roboguru, none of which mention error-type or misconception classification. It is not proof of absence from inside the product (no hands-on subscribed account testing was performed), but the absence is consistent across every primary and secondary source examined.

From ANTARA News (24 March 2022) on the Adapto adaptive feature: 'Di tengah video belajar, guru akan mengecek pemahaman siswa melalui beberapa pertanyaan' ('In the middle of the learning video, the teacher checks the student's understanding through several questions') and, when the student answers incorrectly, 'secara otomatis guru akan memberi pembahasan mengenai jawaban yang benar' ('the teacher automatically gives an explanation of the correct answer'). The remediation is triggered by wrongness alone and delivers the correct-answer walkthrough - there is no branching on WHICH KIND of mistake was made. Separately, Ruangguru's ruanguji page frames its analytics as 'Analisis Pemahaman Materi' that tells the learner 'topik mana yang kamu kuasai dan mana yang perlu pendalaman' ('which topics you have mastered and which need deepening') - topic-level, not error-type-level.

Zenius

Produk Kompetitor Gap
Wilayah Asal: Indonesia (founded 2004 in Jakarta by Sabda PS, Wisnu Subekti, and Medy Suharta;

Zenius is simultaneously the strongest pedagogical brand in Indonesian edtech and its clearest cautionary tale, and on the specific gap question it confirms the same void as Ruangguru. Three findings matter. (1) NO ERROR-TYPE DIAGNOSIS: ZenCore, the most sophisticated adaptive engine any Indonesian edtech has shipped (135,000+ items, 100 levels, three fundamental skill tracks, explicitly weakness-adaptive), still diagnoses only WHICH SKILL is weak, never WHY the answer was wrong. ZenBot reads the question, not the child's working. So the two Indonesian market leaders, from opposite design philosophies - Ruangguru's content-and-scale approach and Zenius's first-principles-and-fundamentals approach - independently converged on topic-mastery as the diagnostic ceiling. That convergence is strong evidence the error-type layer is a genuine unoccupied gap rather than something already quietly solved. (2) PARENT ROLE IS THE ONE PLACE ZENIUS WENT FURTHER: ZeniusLand was designed for SD children to use ACCOMPANIED BY a parent, with parent monitoring - the closest any incumbent came to treating the parent as a participant rather than a dashboard viewer. That it existed proves the parent-co-play model is viable in the Indonesian SD market; that it did not save the company proves co-play alone is not a business model. (3) THE BUSINESS-VIABILITY WARNING: Zenius suspended operations in January 2024 after 20 years, with roughly 30 percent of April 2023 revenue consumed by debt service and a failed fundraise in a tight capital market; it returned in July 2024 as a WEB-ONLY platform, explicitly dropping the mobile app as too heavy and expensive to maintain, and only relaunched an app in July 2026. For a product aimed at Grade 4 children on borrowed Android phones, the operational lesson is direct: heavy native-app infrastructure and a broad content library are the cost structures that killed the most beloved brand in the category. A narrow, deep diagnostic wedge with low content-production overhead is the structurally safer entry, and Zenius's retreat has also left the SD segment thinner than it was in 2021.

Granularitas Diagnosis

topic-mastery-map (skill-level adaptive drilling with levels and points). ZenCore adapts item selection to the learner's weak skills and exposes progress through level, points, and ranking; teacher, government, and parent dashboards show 'hasil belajar siswa' - learning outcomes - so that stakeholders can identify where a student needs additional support. That is topic/skill mastery reporting. It does not reach mcq-distractor-mapped-misconception and is far from a full error-type taxonomy.

Modalitas Input

mcq-distractor (the ZenCore quiz ladder and try-out items are the adaptive signal) + free-response-ocr in a narrow, non-diagnostic sense (ZenBot reads a photographed question via the app or WhatsApp and returns a worked solution with video - it performs OCR on the PROBLEM, not on the child's handwritten attempt, so the child's own working and error steps are never captured or analyzed). Handwriting-stroke capture: absent. Self-report: not a designed mechanism in ZenCore the way Ruangguru's 'Paham/Bingung' button is.

AI Bicara Langsung ke Anak

yes. ZenBot is a student-facing AI assistant reachable 24 hours a day directly by the child through the Zenius application and through WhatsApp: the child photographs a problem and receives an answer plus a video explanation, with no parent in the loop. It is answer-and-explanation oriented rather than conversational-tutor oriented (it is narrower than Ruangguru's AiRIS, which sustains open-ended dialogue and even off-topic chat), but the direct AI-to-child channel unambiguously exists.

Lokalisasi Bahasa

Full native Bahasa Indonesia, and historically Zenius's signature strength was the conversational, first-principles Indonesian explanation style of its tutors (the 'lo-gue' register aimed at teenagers) rather than formal textbook language. ZenBot answers in Indonesian; ZenCore and ZeniusLand are Indonesian-native. Note that the flagship register was designed for teenagers, not for 9-10 year olds, which is a tone mismatch for a Grade 4 audience.

Kekuatan Bukti

moderate for the product-capability findings, strong for the business-trajectory findings. The 'no error-type diagnosis' conclusion rests on convergent negative evidence: the official ZenCore page (zencore.id, fetched directly) describes adaptation to weaknesses with no mention of error classification, and every secondary description of ZenBot frames it as photo-in/solution-out. No hands-on subscribed-account testing was performed, and zenius.net itself returned HTTP 403 to automated fetching, so live 2026 product surfaces were verified only indirectly. The shutdown, the July 2024 web-only return, and the financial pressure figures are corroborated across Tempo, Detik, Infobank, tek.id, and Wikipedia and are reliable.

Bukti Efikasi Remediasi

None found. No RCT, quasi-experiment, or vendor-published efficacy study demonstrating that ZenCore's weakness-adaptive drilling or ZenBot's explanations measurably improve mathematics learning outcomes. Zenius invokes recognized pedagogical frameworks in its marketing - Assessment for Learning and productive failure - but cites them as design rationale, not as evidence that its own implementation works. Combined with the identical absence at Ruangguru, the Indonesian market currently has no incumbent with published proof that its diagnostic layer changes outcomes, which means an entrant that runs even a modest pre/post or quasi-experimental study on error-type-targeted remediation would hold an evidence position no competitor can match.

From the official ZenCore product page (zencore.id): 'Latihan soal yang ada di ZenCore dirancang untuk bisa menyesuaikan pada kelemahan pengguna' ('The practice questions in ZenCore are designed to adapt to the user's weaknesses'), with the stated purpose of concentrating drilling on those weak areas so learning is thorough and efficient. The object of adaptation is the user's weak SKILL AREA, never the user's TYPE OF ERROR. Complementing this, Zenius's own description of ZenBot is that it 'bisa memberikan jawaban dengan mengunggah foto soal, lengkap dengan video pembahasan' ('can give answers by uploading a photo of the question, complete with explanation videos') - confirming that the AI reads the question and explains the correct solution, and never inspects the student's own wrong working.

Quipper

Produk Kompetitor Gap
Moda Layanan: online-app, now predominantly B2B school SaaS. Quipper School / Quipper School P

Quipper is a large, deeply curriculum-localized Indonesian incumbent that is nevertheless absent from the exact gap being probed on all three axes. (1) Error-type diagnosis: no. Despite naming a feature 'Tes Diagnostik' and marketing 'analisis kelemahan', Quipper's diagnosis is a topic-mastery and score map generated from auto-scored MCQ-style items; nothing classifies the KIND of mistake, and the raw output is an .xlsx recap for a teacher to interpret. (2) Parents: no parent surface at all in Indonesia — only teacher and student portals — while the sister Philippine operation does ship a Parents' Portal, showing the omission is a market choice rather than a technical limit. (3) AI tutor to the child: none shipped in Indonesia; the Philippine AI Tutor is still 'COMING SOON', and by design it photographs the QUESTION rather than the child's own written work, so even when it ships it explains problems rather than diagnoses mistakes. Two structural facts sharpen the gap: Quipper retreated from direct-to-consumer in Indonesia entirely when it shut Quipper Video on 1 July 2024, leaving school-mediated B2B as its only route to a child — so a parent of a primary-school child cannot buy Quipper at all; and Quipper's Indonesian content and client base are secondary-school (SMP/SMA) with an SNBT university-entrance centre of gravity, leaving primary-school mathematics (SD, and specifically grades 1-4) unserved. The upshot: Indonesia's most curriculum-aligned LMS incumbent leaves the primary-math, error-type-diagnosis, parent-facing, direct-to-child niche completely open

Granularitas Diagnosis

topic-mastery-map — between score-only and true misconception mapping. Outputs are: (a) numeric grade reports (Laporan Nilai) and answer recapitulations exportable to .xlsx; (b) a student learning profile combining cognitive and affective aspects; (c) tryout reports with topic-level weakness analysis and a probability-style readiness estimate for university admission. No distractor-level misconception mapping is documented, and no error-type taxonomy exists

Modalitas Input

mcq-distractor / auto-graded closed-form items. The Quipper Assessment (Tes Penilaian) workflow is: teacher assigns test to students, students complete within a time window, teacher reads results in Laporan Nilai — a machine-scorable item flow. There is no free-response OCR, no handwriting or stroke capture, and no student self-report of the reasoning step where they got stuck. The Philippine 'AI Tutor' (COMING SOON) does accept a photo, but of the PROBLEM to be explained ('Students take a picture of any problem and receive clear, step-by-step explanations'), not of the child's own written attempt — so even that feature reads the question, not the mistake

Peran Orang Tua

Indonesia: essentially none / passive, mediated entirely by the school. The Quipper Indonesia login page offers only two account types — 'Untuk Guru' (Portal Guru) and 'Untuk Siswa' (Portal Siswa). There is no parent portal, no parent login, and no parent app in the Indonesian product. Community and blog guidance describes parents as collaborating with the homeroom teacher to discuss the child's progress — i.e. the parent receives information second-hand through a teacher meeting, not through the platform. This is a structurally different posture from Quipper Philippines, which explicitly ships a 'Parents' Portal' within the Quipper LMS and markets 'For Parents — Real visibility into progress. Deeper family involvement, with real-time visibility into their child's learning journey — right at parents' fingertips'. Since the B2C Quipper Video shutdown in July 2024, the Indonesian parent is also no longer even the paying customer — the school is

QANDA is therefore best read as the incumbent that validates the market and defines the gap: massive distribution, real AI-to-child conversation, zero error-type diagnosis, zero parent role.

Modalitas Input

free-response-ocr, but critically the OCR is applied to the QUESTION not to the STUDENT'S ANSWER. This is an inverted use of OCR relative to a diagnostic product: QANDA scans printed/typeset problems to look up or generate a solution. A secondary modality exists via the AI chat, where a student can type what they did and be corrected conversationally, but that is unstructured, unlogged as a diagnosis, and depends entirely on the child volunteering their reasoning.

Reliabilitas Diagnosis AI

QANDA is a pure answer-oriented system and therefore sits fully inside that blind spot. Kumon Indonesia

Produk Kompetitor Gap
Wilayah Asal: Japan (method created 1954 by Toru Kumon, a high school math teacher, originallyUsia Target: Prasekolah (preschool, from roughly age 3) through SMA (high school). Pricing isModa Layanan: offline-tutoring, now hybrid. The core is a physical centre (kelas Kumon) attend

Kumon is the trust and parent-relationship benchmark, and simultaneously the clearest demonstration that error-TYPE diagnosis is missing even in the market's most established, most expensive, most parent-integrated math offering. Four points matter for the gap analysis. (1) EVALUATION IS LEVEL-AND-SPEED, NOT ERROR-TYPE: placement sets a starting level; ongoing mastery is judged purely on accuracy plus Standard Completion Time, producing a binary repeat-or-advance decision. The child's error type is never classified, named, tracked, or reported. (2) THE RICHEST POSSIBLE DIAGNOSTIC INPUT IS BEING THROWN AWAY: Kumon physically collects handwritten step-by-step working from millions of children daily — the exact substrate needed to distinguish misreading from concept failure from arithmetic slip — but consumes it through a human grader's eyes and reduces it to a score and a time. Tacit instructor insight exists but is unstandardized, instructor-dependent, and never captured as data. (3) PARENT REPORTING IS REAL BUT GRANULARITY-LIMITED: via monthly orientation, free consultation, and Kumon Connect's Study Records, Indonesian parents already receive scores, completion times, error counts trending over time, and instructor commentary in Bahasa Indonesia. This proves Indonesian parents will pay Rp430,000-575,000 per subject per month and show up monthly for a credible progress narrative — demand for parent reporting is established, not speculative. What they receive is 'how many wrong and how fast', never 'what kind of wrong'. (4) THE KNOWN FAILURE MODE MAPS EXACTLY ONTO THE GAP: the most persistent criticism of Kumon — children who compute fast but cannot do word problems or explain their reasoning — is the predictable outcome of an evaluation scheme that measures speed and accuracy while remaining blind to reading and conceptual error types. Positioning implication: a product that diagnoses error type from the child's own working, reports it to the parent in plain Indonesian, and is mapped to Kurikulum Merdeka would address Kumon's blind spot on all three axes (error type, curriculum alignment, and cost) while borrowing its proven parent-reporting demand.

Granularitas Diagnosis

topic-mastery-map (level placement on the Kumon ladder) combined with trouble-spot-quantity (error counts per worksheet and completion time versus SCT, visible over time in Kumon Connect). It does not reach mcq-distractor-mapped-misconception or full-error-type-taxonomy. The signal a parent receives is 'how many wrong, how fast, which level' — never 'misread the question' versus 'wrong concept' versus 'arithmetic slip'.

Modalitas Input

free-response handwritten worksheet, human-graded. The child writes full working by hand on paper worksheets; the instructor or grader marks them and observes the student working in the centre. Under KUMON CONNECT the same worksheets are completed on a tablet, with instructors and graders able to write comments or underline material directly on the worksheet and send short messages including 'guidance on common mistakes'. This is the richest raw input of any competitor in the set — actual handwritten student working is physically available — yet it is consumed by human eyes and then discarded rather than converted into structured error-type data. Not MCQ-distractor, not OCR, not video-explained, not self-report.

Reliabilitas Diagnosis AI

Not applicable in the AI sense — there is no AI diagnostic engine; judgement is human (pembimbing) and therefore varies with instructor experience and centre quality, with no published inter-rater reliability or validation against ground-truth error labels. The systematic evaluation that does exist is the SCT benchmark, which is statistically derived from international student data and is reliable for what it measures (speed and accuracy) but is by construction blind to error TYPE. Known failure mode of the approach as a whole, widely reported by parents and reviewers: the speed-and-accuracy optimization can produce children who calculate fast but cannot handle word problems or explain their reasoning — i.e. precisely the conceptual and reading-comprehension error types the evaluation scheme never measures. Critics also cite over-reliance on rote repetition limiting conceptual understanding, thin coverage of high-level word problems, and repetition-driven boredom or burnout.

AI Bicara Langsung ke Anak

no — there is no AI tutor and no conversational agent of any kind. All explanation reaching the child comes from (a) the worked examples printed at the top of each worksheet, which are designed so the child can self-learn without being taught, and (b) hints given verbally by the human pembimbing during centre sessions. Kumon Connect adds only asynchronous instructor comments, underlines and short text messages on worksheets — human-authored, not generated. This is the sharpest structural contrast with QANDA, which does chat directly with the child: Kumon has the human trust and the parent relationship but no scalable per-child explanation channel; QANDA has the direct-to-child AI channel but no parent relationship and no diagnosis. Neither has both.

Keselarasan Kurikulum

No — and this is deliberate, not an oversight. Kumon runs an independent, globally standardized curriculum designed to supplement rather than mirror school learning; the same worksheets are used in every Kumon class worldwide. It is not mapped to Kurikulum Merdeka or K13, and the practice of starting students below their school grade means Kumon level and school grade intentionally diverge. Consequence for the gap analysis: a Kumon parent gets a progress signal on Kumon's own ladder that does not tell them how their child is doing against the Indonesian national curriculum their child is actually assessed on at school.

Kekuatan Bukti

strong for pricing, delivery model, placement mechanics, SCT-based mastery criteria and parent-facing features — all verified against Kumon's own official Indonesian and global pages (id.kumonglobal.com fee schedule, id.kumonasiaoceania.com method and FAQ pages, kumon.com Kumon Connect and instructor-role pages). Weak/uncertain for current Indonesia-specific student and centre counts, which rest on a dated academic figure.

Bukti Efikasi Remediasi

Some experimental evidence exists for the METHOD as a whole, but none for error-type diagnosis (which the method does not perform). The strongest is a randomized field experiment in Bangladesh ('Individualized Self-learning Program to Improve Primary Education' / 'Fighting the Learning Crisis in Developing Countries: A Randomized Experiment of Self-Learning at the Right Level', published in Economic Development and Cultural Change) in which primary students received 30-minute Kumon sessions before regular lessons; it reported gains in cognitive ability measured by math test scores and national public examinations, plus non-cognitive gains in self-esteem and grit, and pioneered time-reduction and time-adjusted score measures. A longitudinal study in Pakistan (Global Social Sciences Review) found the Kumon method more effective than traditional lecture instruction for Grade 6 mathematics, with equal benefit for boys and girls. Important caveats for this analysis: these test 'teaching at the right level plus daily repetition', which is the level-placement mechanism, NOT diagnosis of error types; no study isolates a diagnostic component; no Indonesia-specific RCT was found; and the Bangladesh trial was run in a developing-country primary context with a low-performing counterfactual, so effect sizes may not transfer to Indonesian urban SD students. Net: the level-placement-plus-repetition engine has real supporting evidence, while the error-type diagnosis question remains entirely untested because no incumbent implements it.

The 'does not diagnose why' claim is VERIFIED and, more importantly, is structural rather than incidental - which makes Matific the cleanest benchmark for the gap this study is chasing. Matific has arguably the best-engineered prerequisite skill graph in primary edtech (1000+ skills with explicit directed prerequisite edges, a ZPD-targeting engine, a 20-minute placement test, spiral revisiting), it is genuinely multilingual including Bahasa Indonesia, it is priced within Indonesian reach, and it already has at least one real Indonesian school deployment. And it still cannot say whether a wrong answer was a reading error, a conceptual misconception or a calculation slip - because its engine consumes only outcomes, and its remediation vocabulary has exactly one verb: go back to the prerequisite. That single move is correct for a foundational gap and wrong for the other two error types, which is precisely the failure the B/K/H taxonomy is designed to prevent. Second key finding for the Indonesian question: Matific is NOT absent from Indonesia. Bahasa Indonesia is a natively supported language, Indonesia is one of the four target countries of the Matific Foundation's NIKI initiative (Nigeria, India, Kenya, Indonesia), and in 2025 Mentari Group / Mentari Assessment signed an MoU with Sekolah Yehonala in Batam to deploy Matific Math alongside Cambridge English Qualifications. Granularitas Diagnosis

topic-mastery-map, backed by an unusually well-built prerequisite graph - but strictly one level above the granularity that matters. Concretely: (a) a skill network of 1000+ interconnected skills with explicit directed prerequisite edges, where 'An arrow from Skill A to Skill B indicates that Skill A is a prerequisite of Skill B'; (b) a 20-minute placement test on first entry to Adventure Island that sets a baseline; (c) a Student Mastery Report that classifies each topic/skill as 'Mastered' (4+ stars, high probability of success) or 'Not Mastered' (0-3 stars, indicating a potential knowledge gap), drillable from topic to sub-topic to underlying skill; (d) spiral learning / spaced revisiting of previously mastered skills. The output vocabulary is uniformly skills, topics, mastery and gaps. This is a better topic-mastery-map than most competitors - the prerequisite graph genuinely lets it walk BACKWARD to find a missing foundation, which is more than pure difficulty-throttling - but it still answers 'which skill is missing' rather than 'which kind of mistake is this child making'. On the field's own scale it sits at topic-mastery-map, not at mcq-distractor-mapped-misconception and nowhere near full-error-type-taxonomy.

Modalitas Input

Interactive manipulative and game-action input, auto-scored - closest to a richer variant of mcq-distractor but WITHOUT distractor-to-misconception mapping. Children drag, sort, partition, place on number lines, and enter numeric answers inside purpose-built mini-games ('episodes'); Matific's pedagogy is explicitly 'learning by doing' with exploration in a playful setting rather than multiple-choice drill. Crucially, none of the following exist: free-response OCR of a photographed worksheet, handwriting-stroke capture, video-explained reasoning, or self-report of strategy. And although the game layer technically observes the child's interaction sequence, Matific's published engine description consumes only the OUTCOME of that sequence - activity completion and mastery demonstration - not its shape. That is the structural reason the engine cannot classify error type: the process signal is generated but discarded. Contrast with DreamBox, which claims to retain and act on the strategy trace.

Reliabilitas Diagnosis AI

Not applicable in the failure-mode sense, because there is no error-type diagnosis to be unreliable - the honest finding is absence, not error. What Matific does have is an adaptive placement/routing engine whose reliability is validated only indirectly, through downstream achievement studies of the whole platform, never through any ground-truth comparison against expert-labelled misconceptions. Relevant structural weaknesses for this study: (1) the correct-answer trap is fully present - a lucky drag or a guessed numeric entry advances mastery identically to genuine understanding, since only outcomes are scored; (2) the 'Not Mastered' label is a probability-of-failure estimate, so it tells an adult that something is wrong but is silent on what, leaving the diagnostic inference entirely to a teacher or parent who may not have the mathematical-pedagogical training to make it; (3) the remediation move is mechanical - drop to prerequisite skills - which is the right response to a genuine foundational gap and the WRONG response to a reading error or a careless computation slip, yet the engine cannot tell these apart. There is no published validation of the placement test's accuracy, no reported reliability coefficients, and no independent audit of the skill network.

Answering the brief's core question directly: DreamBox's adaptivity goes BEYOND difficulty and topic. Granularitas Diagnosis

Sits between topic-mastery-map and a genuine misconception map, and lands at different points depending on who is looking. Internally, the adaptive engine operates at strategy-trace granularity - it differentiates students who reached the same answer by different routes ('if three different students are working on a particular lesson, they will each be offered different follow-up lessons based on how they responded to questions throughout the lesson'), and reacts to 'repeated errors, hesitation, and inefficient strategies'. Placement is handled by a 'Launchpad placement engine' that starts each student at their just-right level. For TEACHERS, granularity is a standards-proficiency map (Standards Report and Standards Proficiency Report: percentage proficiency per standard, 100% only when every associated lesson is completed with demonstrated proficiency) PLUS Lesson Highlights, which is the one genuinely misconception-adjacent artifact in the product - but it works by showing the teacher the child's actual solution path and letting the TEACHER name the misconception, rather than naming it itself. For PARENTS, granularity collapses to topic-mastery-map at best: progress over week/month/year, lessons completed against a 5-lessons-per-week goal, and whether the child is mastering concepts. On the field's scale: strategy-informed topic-mastery-map for teachers, score-and-mastery for parents, and explicitly NOT a full-error-type-taxonomy for anyone.

Modalitas Input

Interactive virtual-manipulative interaction sequences - a strategy trace - and this is DreamBox's real differentiator. The child works inside purpose-built manipulative environments (number racks, ten-frames, open number lines, area models) where the mathematics is performed rather than selected, so the engine records a sequence of actions and their timing, not a single submitted value. Vendor framing is 'insights from every interaction, not just right or wrong responses'. What is absent: no free-response OCR of a photographed worksheet, no handwriting-stroke capture, no video- or voice-explained reasoning, no self-report of strategy, and no MCQ-distractor-to-misconception mapping (DreamBox is largely not multiple choice, so distractor analysis does not apply). The practical limit: the trace is rich WITHIN the designed manipulative, and blind to anything the child does outside it - notably any working done on paper, which is where an Indonesian SD child's arithmetic slips actually live.

Reliabilitas Diagnosis AI

The misconception-detection claim is vendor-asserted and has never been validated against ground truth in any source found here. The gap is specific and worth stating precisely: DreamBox has strong evidence that the PLATFORM raises test scores (multiple RCTs, an ESSA rating - see below) and NO evidence that its misconception inferences are CORRECT. No published study compares the engine's internal inferences against expert-labelled student misconceptions; no accuracy, precision or reliability figures are published; no taxonomy is published against which accuracy could even be defined. The phrases 'deeper misconception detection' and 'analyzes problem-solving approaches to understand conceptual gaps' appear in marketing and blog material, not in a technical or peer-reviewed description. Relevant failure modes: (1) the correct-answer trap is mitigated but not eliminated - DreamBox can distinguish an efficient strategy from a lucky one within a manipulative, which is a real advantage over answer-only platforms, but a child who guesses correctly inside a simple interaction still looks proficient; (2) hesitation and response-time signals, which the vendor says it uses, are notoriously confounded by distraction, device lag and shared-device conditions - directly relevant to a borrowed-Android use case; (3) the inference is never surfaced, so it is unfalsifiable in practice: no teacher or parent can see the engine's diagnosis and disagree with it, which removes the main real-world error-correction channel. gatekeeper + dashboard-only, with the diagnostic layer deliberately withheld. Reliabilitas Diagnosis AI

The state estimation is the best-validated adaptive assessment mechanism in this competitor set - and that only sharpens the point, because what is validated is mastery localisation, not error-type classification (of which there is none to validate). Validation design: every assessment includes a randomly selected 'extra problem' that is not used to update the state, so its observed correct rate can be compared against the model's prediction; across the dataset the extra-problem correct rate for an item tracks the lucky-guess rate eta_q on outer layers and 1 minus the careless-error rate beta_q on inner layers, confirming the Basic Local Independence Model's predictions - and also showing that beta and eta vary by knowledge-state layer rather than being constant, a known model limitation the authors flag. Documented failure modes relevant to us: (1) the 30-question cap means the assessment usually terminates before convergence, leaving 'uncertain' items excluded from the assigned state, so the initial state systematically UNDERESTIMATES the student's true state (ALEKS compensates by fast-tracking those items in Learning Mode); (2) a documented fatigue effect in long ALEKS assessments (Matayoshi et al. 2018); (3) careless errors are far more common than lucky guesses in ALEKS because items are open-ended, so a mastered item can still be answered wrong and be silently absorbed as noise - the child's slip is modelled away rather than reported to anyone; (4) the update after an incorrect response must be deliberately non-aggressive because of that careless-error rate, which is exactly why the 'I don't know' button had to be added as a workaround. In short: ALEKS knows careless errors exist, measures their rate precisely, and treats them as statistical nuisance rather than as diagnostic information for the teacher or parent.

These two frameworks are the strongest possible theoretical footing for a B/K/H taxonomy, and the state of their operationalisation is the market gap itself. Three findings matter. FIRST, the theory is settled and the empirical distribution of error types actively vindicates a B/K/H design. Clements (1980), analysing 6,595 errors from 634 children, found that only about 25 percent of errors were process-skill (computational) failures and about 5 percent encoding, while 40 percent were reading, comprehension or transformation failures and 30 percent were careless. In other words, roughly seven out of ten mathematics errors are NOT arithmetic errors - they happen before the child ever computes anything. Clements and Ellerton went on to publish this across five countries under the title 'Overemphasising process skills in school mathematics', which is precisely the critique that applies to every drill-and-practice app in this competitive set: they remediate the 25 percent and are structurally blind to the 70 percent. Neither captures the Newman axis. The synthesis for our thesis: the theory says most primary-mathematics errors are reading and comprehension failures; the only commercially operationalised error-type diagnosis covers procedural and conceptual bugs via MCQ distractors or step tracing; and nobody has built the reading/comprehension half, nobody has automated Newman's five prompts, and nobody has ever pointed any of it at a parent.

Granularitas Diagnosis

full-error-type-taxonomy - the reference standard against which every commercial product in this study is being measured. NEA yields a 5-level ordinal classification plus careless/motivation flags (in the NSW Counting On evaluation a sixth category was appended for students who solved the problem successfully, giving a usable 1-6 scale). VanLehn yields a discrete bug library: more than 200 distinct bugs were catalogued for multi-column subtraction alone, with bugs frequently co-occurring in combinations, and each bug is a fully specified procedure so it predicts exactly which future problems the child will get wrong. Critically, Ellerton and Clements (1997) extended NEA beyond wrong answers: analysing responses to 46 questions from Grades 5-8, they 'challenged the view that correct answers equated to understanding', discussed correct and incorrect responses alike, and assigned a Newman error category to a CORRECT answer when the analysis showed it was not accompanied by adequate understanding. That is a 1997 statement of what the 2026 AI-tutor literature now calls the 'correct answer trap', and it is a direct mandate for diagnosing process rather than outcome.

Modalitas Input

self-report / think-aloud interview for NEA; written-work step analysis for VanLehn. NEA's instrument is five scripted prompts delivered orally while the child works: '1. Please read the question to me. If you don't know a word, leave it out. 2. Tell me what the question is asking you to do. 3. Tell me how you are going to find the answer. 4. Show me what to do to get the answer. Talk aloud as you do it, so that I can understand how you are thinking. 5. Now, write down your answer to the question.' Prompt 1 isolates decoding, prompt 2 comprehension, prompt 3 transformation, prompt 4 process skills, prompt 5 encoding. Note the modality implication for a product: prompts 1 and 4 require CAPTURING THE CHILD'S VOICE, and prompt 4 requires capturing intermediate working - neither of which any mastery-map platform (ALEKS, IXL) collects. VanLehn's DEBUGGY works from a different channel: it fits a bug library to the pattern of answers across a carefully designed problem set, requiring no interview at all, which makes it automatable but blind to reading and comprehension failures. A modern implementation would combine them: OCR or handwriting capture of working for the VanLehn axis, plus a spoken or tapped re-tell for the Newman reading/comprehension axis.

Reliabilitas Diagnosis AI

No AI is involved in either original framework - reliability concerns are of two kinds. (A) Intrinsic to the frameworks. VanLehn's own most important negative finding is BUG INSTABILITY and BUG MIGRATION: a bug observed in a child on one occasion frequently fails to reappear on later items or in a later session. VanLehn's explanation is that the underlying incomplete procedure is stable, but the child selects a different REPAIR at the impasse each time, so the surface bug migrates. The practical consequence is severe and directly relevant to us: a single-shot diagnosis of 'this child has bug X' is unreliable, and later work had to introduce probabilistic methods (e.g. Bayesian-network diagnosis of subtraction bugs) precisely to handle instability. Any product claiming an error-type label from one observation is overclaiming; the label needs repeated evidence and an explicit confidence. NEA has its own reliability caveat: it depends on an adult conducting the interview faithfully, and Casey's (1978) modification - where interviewers helped students past each error so a single question could yield multiple error codes - produces classifications that are not comparable with Newman's original single-first-hurdle coding. So even the human gold standard has coding-protocol variance. (B) Extrinsic, for anyone automating this today. Recent work is directly on point: a 2026 study using 20,964 real student responses from the Eedi mathematics platform found that fine-tuned classifiers detect only 57 percent of hidden misconceptions where flawed reasoning produced a CORRECT answer, that standard ML interventions do not improve on this, and that an open-weight reasoning model reaches 84 percent detection but at realistic prevalence produces false alarms outnumbering genuine detections roughly 8 to 1. Separately, LAK 2026 work on automated assessment of handwritten mathematics found LLMs (Gemini strongest) achieve strong-to-moderate performance at diagnosing and classifying student errors against a math-error taxonomy, but that in the best model 87 percent of grading errors were TRANSCRIPTION failures rather than misapplied rubric - i.e. the bottleneck for an OCR-based error-type product is reading the child's handwriting correctly, not reasoning about the error once read.

Peran Orang Tua

none - and this is the single largest unfilled space the two frameworks leave open. Both were built for and by professionals. NEA's interviewer is explicitly a teacher or researcher; the NSW Counting On deployment routed everything through teacher professional learning, classroom posters of the five prompts, and teacher-led remediation, with parents nowhere in the design. VanLehn's audience was cognitive scientists and ITS builders; a bug library is unreadable to a layperson. Neither framework produces any parent-facing artefact, any script for what a parent should say to a child, or any account of how a non-expert adult could run the diagnosis. Yet the five prompts are strikingly parent-executable - they are five plain-language questions requiring no mathematical expertise from the asker, only the willingness to listen. Forty-nine years after Newman, nobody appears to have packaged them for parents. That is a designed-in gap, not a technical barrier.

AI Bicara Langsung ke Anak

no. In NEA the entity addressing the child is a human interviewer following a script; the framework's entire diagnostic power comes from a person listening to a child think aloud. VanLehn's DEBUGGY spoke to no one at all - it produced a bug diagnosis for a researcher or a tutoring system, not an explanation for the learner. The child-facing implementations came later and derivatively: Cognitive Tutor / MATHia surfaces buggy-production feedback messages to the student, but as templated text attached to a recognised buggy rule, not as dialogue. Neither framework specifies, or has ever required, an AI talking to a child - which means an implementation is free to route the diagnosis either to the child or to the parent.

Model Harga

free - both are open academic frameworks in the public literature with no licence, no vendor and no per-seat cost. Newman's five prompts can be printed on a poster (NSW teachers literally did this). The economic implication is that neither framework is defensible as intellectual property; the defensibility of any product built on them lies entirely in the operationalisation - capturing enough of the child's process to classify reliably, and turning the classification into something a parent can act on.

Keselarasan Kurikulum

Not curriculum-bound, and that is an advantage: NEA classifies the PROCESS of solving a word problem, so it overlays any curriculum, including Kurikulum Merdeka and AKM-style numeracy items. Indonesian studies already apply Newman's procedure specifically to AKM content (e.g. analysis of SD students' errors on AKM data-and-probability items using prosedur Newman), which is direct evidence that the framework maps cleanly onto current Indonesian assessment framing without adaptation.

Kekuatan Bukti

strong for the descriptive claim, moderate for the causal claim - and the two must not be conflated. Strong: that mathematics errors decompose into reliably distinguishable types, and that reading/comprehension/transformation failures dominate over computational ones, is supported by thousands of coded errors, cross-national replication across five countries, half a century of use, and an enormous independent Indonesian replication literature. VanLehn's bug catalogue is likewise supported by data from several thousand subjects and was rigorously formalised. Moderate to weak: that ACTING on the diagnosis improves outcomes. The best evidence for NEA remediation is a single-group pre-post evaluation with no control condition (below), and VanLehn's own finding of bug instability undermines confident single-shot diagnosis. The framework's descriptive validity is not in question; its causal payoff has never been tested to modern standards.

Newman's five prompts, verbatim from White (2009): '1. Please read the question to me. If you don't know a word, leave it out. 2. Tell me what the question is asking you to do. 3. Tell me how you are going to find the answer. 4. Show me what to do to get the answer. "Talk aloud" as you do it, so that I can understand how you are thinking. 5. Now, write down your answer to the question.' On the framework's purpose: 'Rather than give students "more of the same" involving drill and practice, NEA provided a framework for considering the reasons that underlay the difficulties and a process that assisted teachers to determine where misunderstandings occurred and where to target effective teaching strategies to overcome them.' On the correct-answer trap, 1997: Ellerton and Clements 'challenged the view that correct answers equated to understanding, thus all responses, both correct and incorrect, were discussed. A correct answer which, after analysis, was not deemed to be associated with an adequate understanding of the main concepts, and/or skills and/or relationships tested by a question, would be associated with a Newman error category, even though the answer was correct.' On the empirical distribution of error types, Clements (1980), 6595 errors from 634 children: '70% of errors belonged to reading, comprehension, transformation or carelessness categories. The first three accounted for 40% and there were approximately 30% careless errors. The mathematics procedure level of process skills registered 25% and encoding only accounted for about 5%.' On VanLehn's mechanism: bugs arise when 'a procedure cannot be performed, an impasse occurs and the individual applies various strategies to overcome the impasse; these strategies (meta-actions) are called repairs' - and systematic bugs 'are not to be confused with slips'. On instability: 'bugs are unstable, meaning that a bug symptom observed in an individual might not show up in subsequent items or in a subsequent testing session.'

Cognitive Tutor / ASSISTments (buggy-rules ITS research)

Kerangka Akademik Ada
Wilayah Asal: Global, USA-originated. Two linked but institutionally separate lineages. (1) ThModa Layanan: online-app, school-mediated and classroom-blended - not a direct-to-consumer par

This tradition is simultaneously the strongest existence proof that step-level error-type diagnosis is buildable and works, AND the strongest published warning that DIAGNOSIS ALONE BUYS NOTHING. Four findings should anchor the thesis. FIRST, and most important as a caution: Sleeman, Kelly, Martinak, Ward & Moore (1989, Cognitive Science 13(4):551-568, 124 citations) ran three controlled studies comparing model-based remediation (MBR) - where the system infers the individual student's specific mal-rules and remediates those bugs - against plain RETEACHING of the correct procedure. MBR was NOT reliably better than simply reteaching. Bunderson & Olsen (1983) had already found no difference between error-specific remediation and reteaching; only Swan (1983), using a CONFLICT approach that actively confronted the student with the consequences of their error rather than merely naming it, beat reteaching. The lesson is precise and directly usable: naming the bug to the learner is not the active ingredient. What differentiates is the remediation ACTIVITY - specifically cognitive-conflict activities that make the error's wrongness visible to the child - not the diagnostic label. A B/K/H product that ships a beautiful taxonomy and a parent report saying 'your child's error was Conceptual' has, on this evidence, shipped the inert half. SECOND, and this is the correction to the framing of this research request: there is NO large-scale ASSISTments RCT showing that error-diagnosis messages fail. The large ASSISTments RCTs are POSITIVE - Roschelle, Feng, Murphy & Mason (2016, AERA Open) found g = 0.18 with 2,850 seventh graders across 43 Maine schools, and the North Carolina replication (63 schools) found sustained effects a year later at the end of 8th grade (effect size around 0.10). What those trials actually show is subtler and more useful: the benefit was attributed to the FULL package - immediate feedback plus hints plus scaffolding plus, crucially, teacher use of the error data to retarget instruction - not to error messages in isolation. THIRD, the ASSISTments team's own meta-analysis of its internal experiment platform makes the diagnosis-versus-remediation contrast quantitatively: Prihar, Syed, Ostrow, Shaw, Sales & Heffernan (EDM 2022, 'Exploring Common Trends in Online Educational Experiments') aggregated 50 randomised experiments covering 30,408 unique students and found that five of six TUTORING strategies significantly outperformed the 'answer only' condition. In other words, telling a student what was wrong is the weak baseline; supplying actual instructional content on top of it is what produces measurable gains. FOURTH, the modality argument in the thesis's favour: VanLehn's (2011, Educational Psychologist 46(4):197-221) meta-review found human tutoring at d = 0.79, STEP-BASED intelligent tutoring at d = 0.76 - statistically indistinguishable from a human tutor - but ANSWER-BASED tutoring at only d = 0.31. Seeing the student's intermediate steps is worth roughly 2.5x an answer-only system. That is the single strongest empirical justification available for capturing handwritten step-by-step working rather than grading final answers, and it comes from outside the AI hype cycle. Synthesised design implication for a B/K/H product: (a) build the step-level capture, because VanLehn shows that is where the effect size lives; (b) do NOT expect the diagnosis label itself to move outcomes, because Sleeman shows it does not; (c) pair every diagnosis with a specific remediation ACTIVITY, preferring cognitive-conflict tasks and contrasting cases that let the child see their rule break, over explanatory messages; (d) re-derive the error taxonomy on Indonesian children's actual work rather than importing one, because Payne & Squibb showed bug libraries do not transfer across populations; (e) keep the taxonomy small and causally organised (B/K/H) rather than a sprawling item-specific bug library, because the sprawling version is exactly what proved unmaintainable and non-transferable; and (f) accept that the entire tradition solved the diagnosis problem for a TEACHER audience and left the parent-delivery problem completely untouched.

Granularitas Diagnosis

Cognitive Tutor / MATHia: full-error-type-taxonomy, and additionally a topic-mastery-map. The buggy production library is a hand-authored taxonomy of named misconceptions (for example a buggy production that fails to distribute an operation fully across an expression), and it operates at the level of individual solution STEPS, not whole answers. Layered on top is knowledge tracing over knowledge components, producing the per-skill mastery bars that drive mastery-learning progression. ASSISTments: trouble-spot-quantity plus topic-mastery-map, with a distractor/common-wrong-answer overlay - the teacher report shows which items and which skills the class struggled with, which specific wrong answers clustered, and Skill Builders enforce mastery thresholds. Critically, neither system reaches the granularity a B/K/H taxonomy needs on ONE dimension: neither separates a READING/comprehension failure from a CONCEPT failure from a CALCULATION slip as an explicit orthogonal taxonomy. Cognitive Tutor's buggy rules are procedural-misconception labels tied to specific problem types; they are numerous and specific but not organised into a small, parent-legible causal taxonomy. That organisational move - collapsing hundreds of item-specific bugs into three actionable causes a parent can act on - is precisely the unoccupied design space.

Modalitas Input

Structured step-entry in a constrained tutor interface - NOT handwriting, and this is the load-bearing limitation. Cognitive Tutor requires the student to type intermediate steps into a worksheet-like grid, algebra tool or equation solver whose input format the model-tracer can parse deterministically. Model tracing only works because the interface forces the student's reasoning into machine-readable slots; the price is that the child must translate their thinking into the tutor's representation, and the tutor sees nothing the interface did not capture. ASSISTments uses typed short answers and multiple choice, with the CWA layer effectively an mcq-distractor / free-response-string clustering. Neither ingests handwriting strokes, neither photographs a child's exercise book, and neither observes the working a child actually produces with pencil on paper. For an Indonesian SD product this matters twice over: kelas 4 children do their real mathematical thinking on paper, and typing structured algebraic steps on a borrowed phone is not a viable input channel. Handwriting-stroke capture of naturally produced step-by-step working is therefore a modality this entire tradition has never used - it gets step-level evidence, which VanLehn's meta-review shows is what actually matters, but it pays for that evidence with an artificial input interface that an elementary home product cannot replicate.

Reliabilitas Diagnosis AI

Well validated as a MECHANISM, but with three documented and severe limitations that a B/K/H product must design around. (1) COVERAGE. Buggy rule libraries are hand-authored by cognitive scientists, one problem type at a time; they are expensive, and they only recognise errors someone anticipated. Any error not in the library is simply 'wrong' with no type attached, which means the diagnostic taxonomy silently degrades to score-only on the long tail. (2) TRANSFER. This is the most damaging finding. Payne & Squibb (1990, Cognitive Science 14:445-481, 'Algebra Mal-Rules and Cognitive Accounts of Error') established that mal-rule / bug libraries DO NOT TRANSFER WELL BETWEEN DIFFERENT POPULATIONS OF STUDENTS - a library calibrated on one group of learners loses much of its explanatory coverage on another. The direct implication for Indonesia is that a bug taxonomy imported from US algebra research cannot be assumed valid for Indonesian kelas 4 arithmetic; it must be re-derived from Indonesian children's actual work. (3) INSTABILITY. The broader literature on diagnostic representation of algebra errors repeatedly finds student errors are less stable across occasions than the bug-library model assumes - the same child may not apply the same mal-rule consistently, which caps achievable diagnostic reliability regardless of engineering effort. This is why the field partly moved to constraint-based modelling (Ohlsson, Mitrovic), which checks violated constraints rather than trying to name the buggy procedure, precisely because enumerating bugs proved brittle. On the positive side, model tracing itself is validated: it is deterministic given the model, and the knowledge-tracing mastery estimates it feeds have been repeatedly shown to predict performance, so the system's failure mode is under-coverage and mislabelling, not fabrication - a meaningfully safer profile than an LLM that will confidently invent a misconception.

Peran Orang Tua

none to passive - the parent is essentially unmodelled in both systems, and this is a structural gap, not an oversight. Both are institution-facing: the data consumer is the TEACHER, not the parent. Cognitive Tutor / MATHia reports go to teacher dashboards and district administrators; Carnegie Learning's implementation model is district professional development. ASSISTments' entire causal story runs through the teacher - the Roschelle et al. (2016) trial explicitly concluded that 'teacher access to student data was an important mechanism' and that teachers 'targeted their homework reviews to focus on student difficulties and errors'. A parent may see a grade; they do not receive a diagnostic report, they are not given remediation scripts, and there is no parent account with its own view. For an Indonesian product where there IS no teacher in the loop and the parent is the only available adult, this tradition supplies the diagnostic engine but supplies nothing at all for the delivery layer - the entire 'who reads the diagnosis and acts on it' problem has to be solved from scratch.

AI Bicara Langsung ke Anak

yes, but non-conversationally, and with an important design lesson. Cognitive Tutor talks directly to the child at every step: it flags the erroneous step immediately, fires the specific buggy message for recognised misconceptions, offers a multi-level on-demand hint sequence escalating from a conceptual nudge to a bottom-out hint that states the answer, and gates progression on mastery. ASSISTments likewise gives the child immediate correctness feedback plus on-request hints and scaffolding sub-questions that decompose the problem. But neither is an LLM chat - all messages are pre-authored and template-selected, so the child cannot ask a follow-up, cannot say 'I still don't understand', and receives nothing outside what an author anticipated. The design lesson from the associated help-seeking literature (Aleven, Roll, McLaren & Koedinger, 'Help Helps, But Only So Much') is that direct-to-child help is not automatically beneficial: students game the system by drilling to bottom-out hints, and help-avoidance and help-abuse are both common failure modes. Putting a tutor voice in front of a child is necessary but not sufficient; what the child DOES with it determines whether it helps.

Keselarasan Kurikulum

None for Indonesia. Both are aligned to US Common Core State Standards and to state frameworks (Carnegie Learning maintains California, Texas and Florida editions and submits to state adoption reviews). There is no Kurikulum Merdeka or K13 mapping, no CP/ATP alignment, no Indonesian textbook coverage and no fase-based sequencing. Their transferable asset for an Indonesian thesis is the METHOD (buggy production rules, step-level model tracing, mastery learning, common-wrong-answer surfacing), not the content.

Kekuatan Bukti

strong. This is the best-evidenced body of work in the entire competitor and framework set. It rests on multi-site cluster-randomised trials with thousands of students (RAND/Pane; SRI/Roschelle), an independent replication with long-term follow-up (WestEd/Feng), What Works Clearinghouse review of Cognitive Tutor, a 50-experiment internal meta-analysis, and a widely cited meta-review of tutoring effect sizes (VanLehn). The cautionary findings are somewhat older and smaller-n (Sleeman 1989 is three studies, not a mega-trial; Payne & Squibb 1990 is a corpus study) and so carry moderate rather than strong individual weight - but they have survived thirty-five years of citation without being overturned, and the EDM 2022 meta-analysis independently reproduces the same directional conclusion with modern data. The one framing that must NOT be asserted is that a large RCT proved error-diagnosis messages fail; the honest and still-powerful claim is that error-specific remediation has repeatedly failed to beat ordinary reteaching, while richer tutoring content reliably beats correctness-only feedback.

Bukti Efikasi Remediasi

Yes, and it is the most decision-relevant evidence in this file - but it points in a direction that constrains the product thesis rather than flattering it. Evidence THAT diagnosis-driven remediation improves learning: weak to null. Sleeman et al. (1989) found model-based remediation no better than reteaching across three studies; Bunderson & Olsen (1983) found no difference between error-specific remediation and reteaching. Only Swan's (1983) cognitive-conflict approach - confronting the student with the consequences of their error - outperformed reteaching, which localises the active ingredient in the CONFRONTATION ACTIVITY rather than in the diagnosis. Evidence THAT the surrounding tutoring system improves learning: strong. Cognitive Tutor Algebra I produced a statistically significant +0.21 SD in high schools in the SECOND year of implementation (equivalent to moving a median student from the 50th to roughly the 58th percentile), with a similar but non-significant estimate for middle schools and NO effect at all in year one - implementation maturity mattered as much as the software. ASSISTments produced g = 0.18 in Maine, replicated in North Carolina with roughly 0.10 persisting a year post-intervention, with larger benefits for lower-prior-achievement students and for minority students, and WestEd's cost-effectiveness analysis found the cost per student very low. The Prihar et al. (2022) 50-experiment aggregation found five of six tutoring strategies significantly better than 'answer only'. VanLehn (2011) puts step-based tutoring at d = 0.76 versus answer-based at d = 0.31. Net reading for a B/K/H product: the diagnosis is the ENABLER, not the intervention. Budget accordingly - the remediation activities, the cognitive-conflict tasks, the contrasting cases and the follow-up practice are where the effect size is bought, and a product that invests 90% of its effort in the diagnostic engine and 10% in what happens after the diagnosis is inverting the evidence. Also note the year-one null in the RAND trial as a realistic expectation-setter: even a well-built system produced nothing measurable until adults had a year to learn to use it.

From Anderson, Corbett, Koedinger & Pelletier, 'Cognitive Tutors: Lessons Learned' (Journal of the Learning Sciences, 1995), the mechanism is stated directly: 'If the student makes a recognizable error (a bug) a message can be given explaining why it is an error. This is generated from a buggy production that embodies the error.' And on the authoring cost: 'Error Messages. When the student makes an error one can present a message that attempts to tell the student something useful about that error. CORRECTION TWO: arXiv 2511.14052 is NOT an EDGE paper. It is 'Making Evidence Actionable in Adaptive Learning' by Mehrabi, Morphew, Quezada & Rebello - and it happens to be MORE useful to this thesis than the paper it was mistaken for, because its entire premise is the gap between 'diagnoses precise' and 'interventions weak'. Its one genuinely useful idea for remediation design is CONTRASTIVE / COUNTERFACTUAL ITEM GENERATION: minimally perturbed items that invalidate a learner's shortcut while preserving psychometric validity, for which the paper establishes conditions under which such items provably reduce the posterior probability of a targeted misconception faster than standard practice. That is the modern formalisation of Swan's cognitive-conflict remediation, and it is exactly the remediation activity the Cognitive Tutor evidence says actually works. NOW THE THREE SUBSTANTIVE FINDINGS. FIRST, final-answer-only diagnosis is not merely imprecise, it is STRUCTURALLY BLIND to an entire error class. When a flawed method yields a correct number there is no observable signal in the answer at all; detection is impossible in principle without a reasoning artifact. This is the cleanest possible refutation of every competitor in the Indonesian market whose 'diagnosis' is built on scoring final answers or on MCQ selection alone. SECOND, the failure is structured and therefore exploitable: 71% of hidden-misconception cases concentrate in two question types with a shared numeric structure, which means an item author can predict and either avoid or deliberately target these traps at design time by checking whether a common wrong method yields the right answer for the chosen values. A B/K/H item bank should do this deliberately - construct diagnostic items where the common misconception and the correct method DIVERGE numerically, so the answer itself carries diagnostic signal, and reserve expensive step-analysis for the residual cases. That is a cheap engineering win nobody in the Indonesian market is taking. THIRD, and most favourable to the handwriting thesis: MalruleLib (arXiv 2601.03217, 6 January 2026) measured the value of step traces directly and found that giving models the student's intermediate STEPS improves misconception prediction by 3-15 percentage points over final-answer-only, across nine models from 4B to 120B parameters. It also found the harder truth - cross-template misconception prediction sits at only about 40%, degrading 10-21 points when the problem template changes, so a model that has learned to spot a misconception in one problem shape largely fails to spot the same misconception in another. Combined with VanLehn's older finding that step-based tutoring reaches d = 0.76 versus answer-based at d = 0.31, the case for capturing handwritten working is now supported from two independent directions, thirteen years apart. SYNTHESISED IMPLICATION FOR A B/K/H PRODUCT: the gap is real and is not closing on its own - frontier research in 2026 still cannot reliably diagnose reasoning from answers, and explicitly recommends keeping a human in the loop. But the same research constrains the product hard: (a) never diagnose from final answers alone; (b) capture the step-level reasoning artifact, and handwriting is the only version of that artifact an Indonesian SD child will produce voluntarily; (c) expect to miss roughly one in six even with the best models, so build for graceful uncertainty rather than confident labels; (d) the false-positive problem is worse than the false-negative problem at low base rates - design a verification probe before ever telling a parent their child has a misconception; (e) separate 'answer correct' from 'method valid' as two explicitly different fields in your data model and in your parent report, because collapsing them is the exact bug that defeats frontier models; and (f) pair every confirmed diagnosis with a contrastive/counterfactual item that makes the child's rule visibly break, since that is the one remediation form with both a theoretical argument (EDGE) and old empirical support (Swan's conflict approach) behind it.

Granularitas Diagnosis

mcq-distractor-mapped-misconception, extended by free-text reasoning classification. Eedi's diagnostic questions map each wrong option to a specific misconception, giving a named-misconception taxonomy rather than a score. The Correct Answer Trap work adds a second, orthogonal axis that is the genuinely novel contribution and that a B/K/H product should copy directly: SEPARATING ANSWER CORRECTNESS FROM METHOD VALIDITY. Modalitas Input

mcq-distractor plus TYPED free-text student explanation - explicitly NOT handwriting, and the gap this leaves is the strongest positive argument in the whole file for a handwriting-based product. The Eedi dataset records, per response: the question, the correct answer, the student's chosen answer, and the student's typed explanation of their reasoning. That typed explanation is what lets a model catch a correct-answer-wrong-method case at all - without it, detection is structurally impossible, because the only observable (the final answer) is correct. MalruleLib works with synthetic step traces rather than authentic student handwriting. EDGE uses distractor patterns and response latencies, i.e. no reasoning artifact at all. Nobody in this literature ingests a photograph of a child's exercise book. The chain of implication for a B/K/H design is tight: (1) final-answer-only diagnosis cannot detect this failure class even in principle; (2) getting the reasoning artifact is therefore necessary; (3) these papers get it by making students TYPE an explanation, which is a heavy compliance burden and, for an Indonesian 9-year-old on a borrowed phone, essentially unachievable; (4) handwritten working is the reasoning artifact children already produce voluntarily and for free, so capturing it is a cheaper route to the same evidence - but no published system does this, and the OCR-of-messy-child-handwriting problem is unsolved and unmeasured in this literature. Kekuatan Bukti

Five findings matter for the B/K/H handwriting-input thesis: (1) The approach is legitimate and current — AIED 2026 (Seoul) accepted a full paper whose entire premise is that correctness signals give 'limited insight into how students regulate their problem-solving', and Kyoto University has a multi-year classroom deployment stack capturing coordinates, velocity, stroke order, pauses and undo/redo/erase events. (2) The ceiling is modest and stroke data is the junior partner: 80.06% accuracy on binary per-step impasse detection, of which self-explanation TEXT alone already delivered 74.0%. Betting a diagnosis engine on strokes alone contradicts the best available evidence; the durable pattern is stroke-timeline as the temporal anchor for a semantic channel (self-explanation text, or the answer content itself). (3) The gap is precisely where the OSN product sits: nobody has mapped stroke features onto a reading/concept/calculation error taxonomy, nobody has done math-process stroke diagnosis with primary-school children (the grade 3-4 pen studies infer cognitive load and emotional valence, not math errors), and no commercial edtech product was found doing stroke-level error-type diagnosis — Mathpix and similar vendors sell handwriting-to-LaTeX recognition, which is transcription, not diagnosis. (4) Hardware reality check for a borrowed low-cost Android phone: the features with the strongest published support are pressure, stroke distance and stroke duration (Oviatt: pressure and distance adapt with expertise, writing SPEED does not), and pressure/tilt need an active stylus. A finger-input phone retains timing, pause-length, stroke-count, correction/erase-event and geometry features — the German primary study's headline signal, pen pressure, would not be available. (5) There is a validated cheaper proxy already in the literature: platform-logged discrete correction events (UNDO/REDO/CLEAR/DELETE memo, page jumps) and duration segments were part of the winning feature set and require no pen sensor at all — arguably the highest-value, lowest-cost signal for detecting a 'Hitung' slip versus a 'Konsep' stall on commodity hardware.

Granularitas Diagnosis

Between trouble-spot-quantity and topic-mastery-map, and short of full-error-type-taxonomy. The most granular published output is per-step binary impasse labels against a hand-authored 4-step rubric of knowledge components (K1-K4) for each problem, temporally anchored to specific stroke numbers in the handwriting replay (e.g. 'Stroke 34: calculated area of triangle ABC — correct step; Stroke 201: expressed coordinates of Q in variables — incorrect step'). AIED 2026 adds a coarser layer: stage-aware regulatory events, question-level dynamics, and stable per-learner behavioral profiles. Nothing in the reviewed corpus emits a named error type.

Modalitas Input

handwriting-stroke, and in the strongest studies handwriting-stroke fused with a second channel. Captured signals across the literature: pen-tip x/y coordinates and velocity vectors, stroke order and count, stroke duration and distance, pen pressure, azimuth/altitude (tilt), pen-up/pen-down ratio and in-air time, inter-stroke pauses and pause duration, total vs. active activity duration ('accurate activity duration', excluding idle intervals), and discrete correction events logged by the platform (UNDO_HW_MEMO, REDO_HW_MEMO, CLEAR_HW_MEMO, DELETE_MEMO, CHANGE_MEMO, PAGE_JUMP). Kyoto fuses these with typed self-explanations embedded via BERT/SBERT and cosine-matched to gold-standard step explanations; HKUST fuses raw trajectories with a Multimodal LLM used as an interpretive layer mapping low-level strokes to functional cognitive operations. Practical constraint for a low-cost Android build: pressure and tilt — the features Oviatt found most predictive — require an active stylus and are unavailable with finger or passive capacitive input; timing, pause, stroke-count, geometry and correction-event features survive finger input.

Reliabilitas Diagnosis AI

Partially validated against human ground truth, with clearly documented and directly relevant failure modes. (1) Best published accuracy for math impasse detection is 80.06% (Nakamoto et al. 2025, hierarchical logistic regression, 900 answers from 93 students across 23 quizzes, 2020-2022), against a human-labeled ground truth in which evaluators reviewed both the handwritten answer and the aligned self-explanations and marked each rubric step correct or incorrect. (2) Critically, the stroke channel is the WEAKER half: a self-explanation-only model already reached 74.0%; adding all handwriting and raw-operational features gained only ~6 points. Digital ink alone is not established as sufficient for step-level diagnosis. (3) The 'correct answer trap' analog is explicitly acknowledged rather than solved: the study adopts VanLehn's definition in which an impasse manifests as being stuck, detecting an error, or doubt 'even when they are technically correct in their actions', and states the approach 'extends beyond the conventional binary evaluation of responses as merely correct or incorrect' to probe students who 'rely on rote memorization or have a cursory understanding'. (4) The authors' own stated limitation is inverse-signal risk: they assume good self-explanations mean no barrier and poor ones signal a problem, but 'not all students are equally skilled at explaining their thoughts in writing, which can lead to misjudging their understanding' — a severe risk for 9-10 year olds with limited writing fluency. (5) Oviatt (ACM TiiS 2018) found average stroke distance, pressure and duration adapt significantly with expertise but writing speed does NOT — a warning that the most intuitive feature (speed) is the wrong one. (6) Handwriting recognition itself is unreliable; Anthony/Yang/Koedinger describe recognition error as 'a clear downside to introducing handwriting input into intelligent tutors', with domain adaptation reducing errors by only 18%. (7) The adjacent dysgraphia-screening literature (Fallah et al., PLoS One 2025, 21 studies) reports 73-99% accuracy but flags small sample sizes, language-specific models, heterogeneous metrics, and that lab performance does not consistently transfer to real settings.

AI Bicara Langsung ke Anak

yes, but thin and school-mediated. The Kyoto EXAIT/stuck-point system automatically detects stuck points and recommends related problems as personalized feedback directly to the learner, and prompts the child to self-explain by replaying their own handwriting timeline and annotating points in time — a direct child-facing interaction loop. It is a recommender plus reflection prompt, not a conversational tutor that explains a misconception in words. No reviewed system has an AI that talks to the child about WHY the error happened.

Model Harga

Not applicable — academic research, not a commercial product. Underlying platforms are institutional and free-to-research (LEAF/BookRoll/LAViEW at Kyoto University); published proceedings are mostly paywalled (Springer LNCS), while the fullest methodological paper (Nakamoto et al. 2025, RPTEL) is open access CC-BY.

Bukti Efikasi Remediasi

Weak to absent — this is the sharpest hole in the entire literature. No RCT or quasi-experiment was found showing that stroke-derived or process-derived diagnosis actually improves mathematics learning outcomes. The closest study, the AIED 2025 Kyoto pilot with ~80 middle-schoolers over roughly one month, reports only that the system 'was capable of automatically detecting stuck points from self-explanations and recommending related problems as personalized feedback' — a feasibility and detection result, with no reported learning gain against a control. Nakamoto et al. (2025) is purely a detection-accuracy study. Oviatt's expertise-prediction work is correlational. The one genuine controlled comparison in the adjacent space concerns input modality rather than diagnosis: Anthony, Yang & Koedinger found students solved algebra problems roughly twice as fast with handwriting than with typing, made fewer entry errors, showed increased transfer to non-computer tasks, and 'seem to learn just as much as their typing counterparts' — i.e. handwriting input is a defensible UX choice with an efficiency benefit, but that is not evidence that stroke-based DIAGNOSIS remediates anything. Separately, a large RCT does exist for LLM-generated feedback helping low-knowledge middle-school math students with short-term learning (ACM L@S 2026), suggesting the feedback-delivery half of the loop has independent support even though the stroke-diagnosis half does not.

Keselarasan Kurikulum

Kurikulum Merdeka is the governing frame and it creates genuine policy tailwind because it explicitly mandates asesmen diagnostik (diagnostic assessment) at the start of learning, defined as identifying learner capability 'agar guru dapat merancang pembelajaran yang sesuai' (so the teacher can design appropriate instruction). The tailwind is unrealised in practice: Indonesian studies of SD teacher competency find teachers 'masih mengalami kesulitan dalam menyusun instrumen asesmen yang sesuai' (still struggle to construct suitable assessment instruments), and systematic literature review of mathematics teachers implementing Kurikulum Merdeka finds multiple persistent implementation barriers. So the curriculum has already legitimised and named the exact job-to-be-done (diagnose before you teach) while leaving the instrument unbuilt. Notably, the premium offline providers deliberately run NON-aligned curricula: Sparks Math sells the Singapore curriculum with CPA (Concrete-Pictorial-Abstract) methodology and Bar Model, and Sakamoto and Kumon likewise sell imported methods, indicating parents treat local-curriculum alignment as optional when a method carries stronger perceived diagnostic credibility.
